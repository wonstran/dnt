# Response to the follow-up review of the vehicle reversal split design

Design: [2026-11-04-vehicle-reversal-split-design.md](superpowers/specs/2026-11-04-vehicle-reversal-split-design.md) (now rev. 3)

All three findings are correct and are accepted. I reproduced each one with production code before changing the design. Two claims in my earlier response are withdrawn:
- that the variable-height test could take its parts from the new rule and still show the re-link;
- that a chain across a cut needs screen to drop rows first.

## Changes

| Comment | Resolution | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **[P2]** Pin the historical cut in the variable-height regression | Took your first option. The test now has two parts. **Isolated link regression:** the fixture is split by hand at **23**. It must re-link without `forbidden_cuts` and must not with `{1: [23]}`. **Rule and pipeline:** the same fixture must give hit run 23–25, a cut at **25**, and two output tracks from `TrackRefiner`. That part says explicitly that link already refuses the join at 25, so it tests the stage's output, not the guard. | §6.2 |
| **[P2]** Check parity before cut-dependent length filtering | Parity is now on the raw hit rows. A helper runs the script's code unchanged up to `hit = np.flatnonzero(cos <= cos_max)`, and the stage exposes the same intermediate, `reversal.hit_rows`; the two must be equal exactly. The cut chosen in each run and the step 7 length check are tested on the stage's own rows, against an independent reference implementation. Kept cuts are not compared with the oracle. §3.2 now splits the rule into runs (step 5), the cut (step 6) and lengths (step 7). It records your near-start example as the reason kept runs can differ even with one reversal. The near-start fixture (cut at 5, kept) and a mirrored near-end fixture (run 23–27, cut at 25, kept) are pinned. **Not done as asked: a pinned spacing fixture.** I searched for one and found none (see below), so §6.3 records the search and relies on the reference check of step 7, which covers spacing whatever cuts the stage picks. | §2 criterion 2, §3.2, §6.1, §6.3 |
| **[P2]** The chain limitation does not need dropped rows | I reconsidered the scope rather than only fixing the explanation. Once a chain is possible on untouched sides, leaving it out would break the stage's main promise whenever a duplicate box sits at the turn. Since the review did not ask for this, here is the full change. One predicate, `straddles(lineages_x, lineages_y, cuts)`, drives three checks: (1) link pairs, as in rev. 2; (2) a new `resolve_reversal_chains` after `resolve_chains` (and on legacy-mode accepted events), which drops the weakest link, by `(algo_score, proposal_key)`, on the chain path between the two sides, marked `skipped_reason = "reversal"`. Links in the chain that are not on that path are never dropped. (3) A fourth reason, `"reversal"`, in dedup's `blocker`, because a long duplicate track can join both sides through two accepted `MERGE` events just as `X` does through two links. Your fixture is a pinned test, with the failure shown first, along with an unrelated link that must survive, legacy mode, and a dedup component. | §2, §3.5, §6.2, §8, success criterion 4 |
