# Response: review of track refinement design rev. 5

Responding to: [review_2026-09-28_11-05-56.md](review_2026-09-28_11-05-56.md)

All four comments are accepted.

One terminology change comes with comment 1. Stage 3's repeated Hungarian assignments are now called **passes** (`link.max_passes`, `signals.pass`), and "round" means only a review round (`Event.round`). Previously the spec used "round" for both.

---

1. **High: freeze accepted links when re-assignment runs.** **Accepted.**

   - **Pass rules** (§6.3, "Re-assignment after rejection"). Each pass after the first works on a reduced graph:
     - rejected edges are removed;
     - **accepted pairs are frozen**: kept as they are, with both endpoints removed from the graph;
     - endpoints of `HUMAN_PENDING` edges stay reserved, as before.

     A later pass therefore cannot re-assign a verified link's endpoint to a different partner, and the ledger cannot hold two accepted links that conflict.
   - **Replay.** Stage 3 re-runs under the same rules: recorded decisions are applied by key, pass by pass. A person who rejects a previously accepted link frees its endpoints in the same way. The pass number is not part of `proposal_key`, so a pair keeps its key whichever pass proposes it.
   - **Test** (`test_link.py`): a component where a→x is accepted and b→y is rejected, and where the unconstrained pass-2 optimum would be b→x plus a→z.
     - a→x stays applied, x is not offered to b, and pass 2 proposes b's best partner other than x, or nothing.
     - No two accepted links share an endpoint.
     - The same is checked on replay with b→y rejected through `apply --decisions`.

2. **High: the orphan pass can drop a fragment whose link is pending.** **Accepted.** Chosen approach: the orphan decision **waits for** the link decision.

   - **Deferral** (§6.2, orphan pass). A track that is an endpoint of a `HUMAN_PENDING` `LINK` is skipped, not scored, and is listed in the summary under `orphan_deferred`.
   - **Resolution.** When the reviewer decides the link, stage 3 re-runs because it owns a changed event (the rev. 5 replay rule), and the orphan pass re-runs after it:
     - a rejected link makes the fragment an orphan candidate again;
     - an accepted link makes it part of a longer track.
   - **Tests:**
     - `test_screen.py`: a 0.1 s fragment with a pending link gets no orphan event and appears in `orphan_deferred`.
     - `test_replay.py`, end to end: the fragment stays in the output while pending. After `apply` rejects the link, `DROP{orphan}` is proposed and auto-accepted. After `apply` accepts it, the fragment is linked and no orphan event is proposed.

3. **High: replay without the video needs a rule for a missing embedding cache.** **Accepted.** The feature cache is now a **verified replay input**.

   - **Recorded in the ledger.** The header's `inputs` gains `features: {path, sha256, cache_key}` (§4.2), and `apply` gains `features=` / `--features` as a location override.
   - **Complete by construction** (§5.3). The embedded samples are the coarse samples plus the dense samples around every stage 1 candidate, whatever its decision. They depend only on the raw inputs and on stage 1's proposals, which never change on replay. So a cache written by `refine` covers every embedding any replay can request, including the boundary samples of tails from splits accepted later.
   - **Rule** (§4.2). If stage 1 or stage 3 will re-run and `encoder.kind` is not `none`, `apply` needs either:
     1. the recorded cache, present with a matching SHA-256 and `cache_key`, or
     2. the verified video, from which the embeddings are recomputed.

     If neither is available, `apply` raises `ValueError` before processing. The message names both paths and says to restore either one or pass `--video` / `--features`. The case of a missing video with a valid cache is unchanged: a warning, and evidence-less `HUMAN_PENDING` for new events.
   - **Tests** (`test_replay.py`):
     - With the video and the cache both removed, `apply` accepting a pending `SPLIT` raises `ValueError` naming both.
     - With only the video removed, it succeeds from the cache.
     - With the video removed and the cache modified, it raises a hash mismatch.
     - With the cache removed and the video present, it recomputes, and the output equals the run that used the cache.

4. **Medium: state that pending events are serialized.** **Accepted.**

   - **Every proposed event is written** (§4.2), including `HUMAN_PENDING` ones, with its current decision. A stage writes its events once its verification is done (for stage 3, after its last pass). The old wording, "written when its decision is final", is removed.
   - **Ledgers are never edited in place.** `apply` writes a new ledger with the complete current state:
     - The header records `parent: {path, sha256}` and `round`.
     - Events matched by `proposal_key` keep their `id` across rounds, so a reviewer's exported IDs stay valid. Only new events get new IDs.
     - Each event carries `decision` and a `decision_history` of `{decision, round, source}` entries (§4.1).
   - **Test** (`test_replay.py`, pending event round trip):
     1. `refine` leaves a `LINK` pending.
     2. The ledger holds it as `HUMAN_PENDING` with an `id`, and it deserializes to an equal `Event`.
     3. A `decisions.json` for that `id` is passed to `apply`.
     4. The new ledger keeps the `id`, shows `HUMAN_ACCEPT` with two history entries, names its parent, and has `round: 1`. The parent ledger is unchanged, byte for byte.
