# Response to the review of revision 4 of the vehicle reversal split design

Design: [2026-11-04-vehicle-reversal-split-design.md](superpowers/specs/2026-11-04-vehicle-reversal-split-design.md) (now rev. 5)  
Review: [review_2026-11-04-21-56-20.md](review_2026-11-04-21-56-20.md)  
Earlier: [response_2026-11-04_22-10-00.md](response_2026-11-04_22-10-00.md)

The finding is correct and is accepted. Rev. 4 described `absorb` as carrying "the tags of each absorbed track into its representative". `MergeOutcome.absorbed` has no entry for a representative, so a literal implementation would have lost `(1, 0)` in the very replacement case the design uses as its example. The rev. 4 example and pipeline test stated the right outcome; the helper contract did not produce it.

## Change

| Comment | Resolution | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **[P2]** `absorb` must also keep the representative's own pre-merge tags | The contract now says that `tags_before` is a snapshot of `own ∪ carried` for **every** track before the merges, the representatives included, and that the transfer is your loop: `carried[mapping.get(t, t)] |= tags_before[t]` for every `t`. It explains why tags carried by dedup survive a later link map: `tags_before[t]` already contains `carried[t]`, and tags are only ever added. The link call names its snapshot (`tags_before_link`, every track on the table link received). The minimal case `mapping = {3: 1}`, `tags_before = {1: {(1, 0)}, 3: ∅}` is a unit test that requires `(1, 0)` in `tags(1)` while track 1's lineage gives no tag. | §3.5 (`absorb` contract, *Link* point), §6.2 (*Side tags* unit test), §12 |

## What I ran

Nothing new. The finding follows from the shape of `MergeOutcome.absorbed`, which I read in `dedup.py` (`absorbed` maps absorbed IDs to representatives), and from the dedup run in my previous response: track 1 was left holding only raw 3 after the merge. The review's own production check confirms `out.absorbed = {3: 1}` on that fixture.
